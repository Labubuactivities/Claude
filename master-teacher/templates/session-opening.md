# Session Opening Template

The standard opening protocol for any master-teacher session. The teacher runs this; the student doesn't need to memorize it but should recognize the pattern after a few sessions.

-----

## If this is the first session in a domain

### 1. Goal calibration (5 min)

- "What's your specific goal for this domain? Not 'get good at investing' — the actual operating goal."
- "What would 'mastery' look like for you? What would you be able to do that you can't do now?"
- "What's your background relevant to this? What have you already read or done?"
- "What's the time budget — how many sessions, over what period, with what intensity?"

This conversation produces the top of `templates/syllabus.md`. Don't skip it. Teaching without a goal is unfocused, and unfocused teaching is fluff regardless of how much content gets covered.

### 2. Diagnostic (10 min)

Run the diagnostic protocol from `references/09-diagnostic-protocols.md`:

- "What do you currently think you know about this domain?"
- "Where do you think you're most uncertain or confused?"
- "Give me a specific case where this matters to you."

Calibrate the starting stage from the answers (Stage 1-4).

### 3. Council preview (5 min)

- "Here's the council we'll be working with. Here's where they sharply disagree. The contested questions you'll be able to argue both sides of by the end of Stage 3."

The student doesn't need to memorize this now. The exposure matters because it signals what the curriculum is structured around.

### 4. First substantive content (the rest of the session)

Begin Stage 1 orientation with the first real topic. End the session with retrieval and an assignment.

-----

## If this is a subsequent session

### 1. Retrieval drill (5-15 min)

Run the spaced-retrieval protocol from `references/11-spaced-retrieval.md`:

- "Before we start: walk me through what we covered last time. Not a summary — the substantive content. Concepts. Council positions. Where they disagreed. The case we used."

Let the student talk. Listen for:

- Gaps in retrieval (specifically what didn't stick).
- Bluffs in retrieval (fluent paraphrase without substance).
- Mis-rememberings (what they think we covered vs. what we actually covered).

If a retrieval fails, work the failure rather than bypassing it. Failed retrieval is the highest-value teaching moment in a session.

Then test a topic from 2-3 sessions ago (interleaving). Then test a topic from many sessions ago. Vary the question types (definition, mechanism, master/position, counter, case, application, connection, gap).

### 2. Calibration check (2-3 min)

- "How did that feel? What's solid, what's slippery, what's gone?"

The student's self-report is data. Combine with their actual responses to determine where remedial work is needed before new content can land.

### 3. Plan for today (1-2 min)

State briefly what this session will cover:

> "Based on the retrieval and the syllabus, here's what I want to do today: [the plan]. Push back if that's wrong."

Contractual move. Sets expectations and lets the student redirect if the calibration is off.

### 4. Today's content (the bulk of the session)

The session arc per `references/14-style-modes.md`:

- Diagnostic if entering a new sub-topic.
- Lecture-block if filling prerequisite content (kept short).
- Socratic for derivation.
- Case study for application.
- Debate for pressure-testing.
- Closing Socratic for retrieval of what was just covered.

### 5. Synthesis (5 min)

- "How does what we covered today connect to [prior sessions]?"
- "How does it connect to [other domains the student studies]?"

The student does the connecting work. The teacher fills gaps.

### 6. Assignment (3-5 min)

Specific assignment for between sessions. Three components:

- **Reading:** specific work and specific section.
- **Thinking:** a specific question to sit with.
- **Doing:** if applicable, an exercise, case, or journal entry.

Plus a re-retrieval flag: which topics from today to quiz on in 3 days, 1 week, 1 month.

### 7. Log entry (after the session)

Update `templates/progress.md` for this session. Update `templates/syllabus.md` for any curriculum changes.

-----

## What the student needs to know

After 2-3 sessions, the student should recognize this structure and be able to:

- Anticipate the opening retrieval and prepare for it.
- Request mode shifts when they need them ("can we drill on this for a few minutes?").
- Self-diagnose where they are in the curriculum.
- Push back when the calibration is wrong.

A student who can run their side of this protocol is taking ownership of their own teaching, which is the goal.
