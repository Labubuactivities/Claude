# Post-Session Reflection Template

A short reflection the student does on their own, ideally within 24 hours of each session. The function is dual:

1. **Consolidation.** Writing forces retrieval, which strengthens memory more than re-reading or passive review.
1. **Self-monitoring.** Tracking your own reactions and gaps over time produces metacognitive data that no teacher can produce for you.

Keep this short. 10-15 minutes per session, not 60. Long reflection logs that no one rereads have no value; short reflection logs that you actually revisit are gold.

-----

## Reflection: Session [N] — [Date]

### One-paragraph recap (in your own words)

[Write a single paragraph, 4-8 sentences, summarizing what we covered. Do this from memory, not from the progress log. The act of retrieving and rewriting is the work. Then check your paragraph against the progress log — what did you miss? What did you misremember? Note both.]

### The one thing I most clearly understand now that I didn't before

[Specific. Not "I understand X better." More like: "I now understand why Buffett's framework systematically failed on platform businesses in the 1990s — it's because his moat doctrine was derived from physical-asset and brand-based businesses where the moat mechanism was visible ex ante, and platform moats are emergent from network effects that only became identifiable in retrospect."]

### The one thing I thought I understood but actually don't

[Be honest. The session probably surfaced at least one bluff or misconception. Name it specifically.]

### Where I felt myself bluffing during the session

[The moments where I gave a confident-sounding answer without real substance underneath. What was I bluffing about? Why? What's the actual gap I was covering up?]

### Where I pushed back productively or successfully defended a position

[The moments where the teacher's attack failed because I had real reasoning. What was the position? What was the attack? Why did the defense hold?]

### Questions I'm sitting with

[Questions that came up that haven't been resolved. Carry these forward. Bring them to next session if they still feel live.]

- [Question 1]
- [Question 2]

### Connections to other things I know or do

[How does today's content connect to other things — my work, other domains I study, decisions I'm currently making? This is where teaching becomes useful in life. Force the connection explicitly even if it feels strained.]

### Confidence calibration check

For each of the major claims I made or accepted during the session, what's my actual confidence?

- **[Claim 1]:** [Level 1-4]. [What would change my mind?]
- **[Claim 2]:** [Level 1-4]. [What would change my mind?]
- …

(Sometimes the honest answer reveals that I'm holding a "Level 1" claim that's actually Level 3 once I push myself.)

### What I want to do differently next session

[Specific. Not "engage more." More like: "Spend the first five minutes of the session reading my notes from the last two sessions before the retrieval drill — I keep failing the early-session retrieval because I'm cold." Or: "When I feel myself starting to bluff, stop and say 'I'm not sure' instead. The bluff doesn't help me, it just hides the gap."]

### Assignment I committed to

[Restate the reading, thinking, and doing assignments from end-of-session. Set a specific time to do each.]

- **Reading:** [work, sections] by [date]
- **Thinking:** [question] for [time period]
- **Doing:** [exercise/case/journal] by [date]

-----

## Periodic deeper reviews

Every 5-10 sessions, do a longer review (30-45 min):

### What patterns am I seeing in my own thinking?

- Where am I systematically overconfident?
- Where am I systematically underconfident?
- Which masters' frames do I default to too easily? Which do I avoid?
- What kinds of cases do I analyze well? What kinds do I avoid?

### How is the curriculum trajectory going?

- Am I in the right stage? Too easy, too hard, or right?
- Are the cases landing or feeling abstract?
- Is the retrieval performance trending up or sideways?
- What would the version of me 6 months from now thank me for working harder on now?

### What needs to change?

- Curriculum adjustment.
- Reading queue update.
- Pace change.
- Domain priority shift.

Bring this to the next session and discuss with the teacher.

-----

## What this is not

- Not a journal of feelings. Useful only insofar as it surfaces actionable patterns.
- Not a recap exercise to perform for the teacher. The teacher doesn't read this (unless you choose to share it).
- Not optional in the sense that "I'll skip it this week." Skipping consistently corrupts the curriculum because the consolidation work doesn't happen and the diagnostic patterns become invisible.

The serious student does this. The not-yet-serious student doesn't. The line between them is largely the line between students who eventually develop mastery and students who keep taking lessons forever without trajectory.
